# Bluon Voice Bank — mined from the 2022–23 Coda archive

*Source: Tanner's old Coda "Marketing For Review" doc (grid `Content for Review`, 160 rows,
~60 sent text/email/push campaigns from Jul 2022 – Feb 2023). Scraped Aug 7 2026. This was
the era Peter and Tanner point at when they say "how we used to message." The Sunday topic
generator feeds sections of this file into its prompt; humans drafting in the machine should
read it once.*

> ⚠️ **Reuse the VOICE, never the MECHANICS.** Everything below references 2022-era product
> mechanics that are DEAD or changed: BluonPro signups, 2% cashback / Bluon Points, parts
> *ordering/quotes* through the app, free 24/7 tech support (Live Tech Support is a PAID
> standalone product now — and never lives inside an FSM integration), supplier contests.
> Copy the energy, sentence shapes, and humor. Verify every feature claim against today's
> product before shipping anything.

## The voice in one paragraph

Talk like a tech texting another tech, not a brand emailing a lead. Name first with an
exclamation ("Hey Mike!"). Short lines, one idea each. One or two ALL-CAPS words for punch,
never more. Swear-adjacent is fine and on-brand ("pain in the ass", "No BS!", "What the hell
are you waiting for?"). Big specific numbers beat adjectives ("68,150 boilers & furnaces
ADDED!" not "tons of new equipment"). Meme and GIF references carry the joke. Close warm and
loyal: **"We've got your back!" / "Bluon has your back," then "-The Bluon Team" or "-Bluon"**
— that sign-off pair is THE signature move, used on almost every send.

## Format norms (what actually shipped)

| Format | Shape | Length | Historical timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Text | greeting → one idea → payoff → link/GIF | 68–164 chars for blasts (never over ~400) | Wed 3pm per time zone |
| Email | subject w/ hook → "Hey <name>," → 3–6 short lines → GIF/video → sign-off pair | way under 200 words | Sat 9am per time zone |
| Push | Line 1 hook (imperative or question, ~25 chars) → Line 2 payoff/tease | 2 lines total | Thu or Mon morning |

Weekly cadence then: 1 text + 1 email + 1 push to the whole tech base, segmented into
Groups 0/1/2/3 by product state — same skeleton per group, ONE line varies. (Today's
equivalent: Engaged / Unengaged.)

## Push formula — hook + payoff, verbatim examples

- "UPDATE YOUR APP! / Your day would've been so much easier."  ← the gold standard
- "Stuck on a Job? / Call Bluon Tech Support!"
- "Supplier Doesn't Have Your Part? / Have them find replacements on Bluon!"
- "Start w/ Model # / And make your job easier!"
- "Earn 2% Back / Get Quotes, Get Paid"
- "MAJOR Forum Upgrade! / Getting help is EASIER!"
- "1,500 Suppliers on Bluon! / Find yours in the App."

## Subject line patterns, verbatim

- **Number flex:** "68,150 Boilers & Furnaces Added" · "1,500 Suppliers Now on Bluon!" · "TONS of Suppliers ADDED!"
- **Anti-corporate honesty:** "Tech support that doesn't SUCK" · "OEM Tech Support Sucks!" · "How does Bluon make $$$???" · "You said it, Not us"
- **Pop-culture bridge:** "First Chipotle + Starbucks → Now HVAC Parts!!" · "Finally! HVAC gets its 'Uber' Revolution!"
- **Tease:** "This Could be You..." · "NEVER AGAIN: WRONG PARTS"
- **Seasonal pun:** "It's Getting Hot in Here!" · "Heating season just got cool." (body close)
- **Meme-forward:** "Florida man gets HVAC parts EASILY!"

## Sentence shapes that repeat

- Numbered dead-simple: "1) Get a quote. 2) Get it approved. 3) Get paid! 4) It's that easy!"
- The "No BS" flex: "EARN $100's on the BLUON APP! No BS!"
- Identity flattery: "our badass tech support team is ready to help you through heating season 24/7/365!"
- Pain-first opener: "Trying to find a part #? Can't read or find the number? We know, it can be a huge pain in the ass!"
- Weather empathy: "<name> It's freaking hot out there!"
- Blunt imperative stack: "UPDATE your Bluon app! DO IT NOW!"
- Irreverent close: "Now go buy some cool shit." (points redemption email — approved & sent)
- Owning a screwup: "Houston we have a problem! Looks like the heat got to us, and we gave you a messed up list - We are human after all!"
- Hype cadence: "Get focused. Get hyped. Get cash!"
- PS tease: "P.S. For really good videos submitted we have some VERY cool surprise gifts!"

## Humor / GIF playbook

GIFs and memes carried the joke; copy stayed the straight man. Referenced in the archive:
Dave Chappelle "I'm rich" for points, mad-Joe vs happy-Joe for OEM vs Bluon support, the
Staples "Easy Button" ("The Easy Button just got Real!"), Florida-man framing, old-school
cartoon GIFs, office-dog-in-a-bucket-hat (Sunny). Texts CAN carry a small GIF but Twilio
today strips anything big — when in doubt, no image, and never let the joke depend on it.

## Engagement plays that worked (adapt, don't clone)

- **Feedback bribe:** shoot a 30-sec video, get a bucket hat ("You give us feedback, we give
  you a Bluon bucket hat."), PS surprise gifts for great ones. High-warmth, cheap.
- **Transparency content:** "How does Bluon make money?" video — trust builder, techs loved it.
- **Supplier/big-number news** as momentum proof.
- **Service notices with personality:** even "tech support is offline" got the party framing
  ("We are all getting together to train a little and party a lot! Even WE need to let loose
  every once in awhile! Cheers!").
- **Peter-signed asks** for owner/contractor audiences (signed "-Peter Capuciati", sent from
  peter@bluon.com) when the ask was feedback from decision-makers.

## What today's copy must NOT inherit

- 2% cashback / Bluon Points / gift-card redemption — dead.
- "100% free" tech support claims — Live Tech Support is paid + standalone now.
- BluonPro signup pushes, supplier contests, parts *ordering* flows — 2022 mechanics.
- "100,000+ techs" in B4B copy — use "thousands of contractors" per current rule.
- Em-dashes and semicolons in email copy (current house rule; the old archive predates it).
